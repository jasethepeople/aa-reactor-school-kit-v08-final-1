Lesson 1: Gas Evolution — Vinegar + Baking Soda

GREEN — Safe supervised hands-on with goggles. Materials: 5% white vinegar, baking soda, beaker, spoon, goggles.

Objective

Observe gas evolution (CO₂), conservation of mass, acid + base → salt + water + gas.

Equation (Real)

NaHCO₃ + CH₃COOH → CO₂ + H₂O + NaCH₃COO (sodium acetate) Balanced with spectator: NaHCO₃ + CH₃COOH → Na⁺ + CH₃COO⁻ + CO₂ + H₂O

App Steps

  • Open v0.4.1 → REAL mode → Library: NaHCO₃ + CH₃COOH → Run
  • Watch bubbles, log says exothermic/endothermic, mass conserved

Physical Demo (optional, with teacher)

  • Put on goggles, gloves
  • 50mL vinegar in beaker, 1 tsp baking soda
  • Observe fizz, listen, feel beaker (slightly cool = endothermic)
  • Mass before/after? CO₂ escapes, mass appears to drop — discuss closed vs open system

Questions

  • Where did bubbles come from? (CO₂ gas)
  • Why does mass seem to decrease in open beaker? (gas leaves)
  • What are reactants/products?

NGSS

PS1.B: Chemical Reactions — new substances formed. PS1-2: Analyze data.

Lesson 2: Exothermic & Endothermic — Neutralization

YELLOW — Teacher demo only. Materials: dilute HCl 0.1M, dilute NaOH 0.1M (teacher handles), beakers, thermometer, goggles, gloves, ventilation.

Objective

Feel exothermic: acid + base → salt + water + heat. Temperature rises.

Equation

HCl (aq, dilute) + NaOH (aq, dilute) → NaCl + H₂O + heat (-57 kJ/mol) Net ionic: H⁺ + OH⁻ → H₂O

App Steps

  • REAL mode → HCl-dil + NaOH-dil → Run → orange glow = exothermic, beaker warm
  • Show energy bar: -57 kJ/mol released

Teacher Demo

  • Teacher wearing PPE mixes equal small volumes (20mL each) of 0.1M dilute
  • Students observe thermometer rise 2-4°C, feel outside of beaker (warm)
  • Never use concentrated — only 0.1M dilute, teacher handles

Teaching Note

Exothermic = releases heat (feels warm). Endothermic = absorbs heat (feels cold). Neutralization is classic exothermic.

Lesson 3: Precipitate — White Solid Forms

GREEN — Safe supervised. Materials: table salt solution, chalk dust or baking soda solution, clear cups.

Objective

Observe precipitate formation — insoluble solid forms from soluble ions.

Equation (simplified safe version)

NaCl (aq) + CaCO₃ (suspension) → CaCl₂ (aq) + Na₂CO₃ (aq) — with AgNO₃ version: AgNO₃ + NaCl → AgCl (s) white precipitate + NaNO₃ App uses safe analogue to avoid AgNO₃ handling.

App Steps

  • REAL mode → NaCl + CaCO₃ → Run → crystals grow from bottom, cloudy then settling
  • Log: precipitate formed

Physical

  • Mix clear salt water + clear baking soda water — observe slight cloudiness if hard water
  • Better: use app to show ideal, discuss water treatment, kidney stones as precipitate analogy

Lesson 4: Slow Oxidation — Rusting

GREEN — Safe. Materials: steel wool, water, air, magnifying glass (optional). Takes days.

Objective

Oxidation doesn't have to be fire — rust is slow combustion.

Equation

4Fe + 3O₂ + 6H₂O → 4Fe(OH)₃ → Fe₂O₃·nH₂O (rust) — slow exothermic

App Steps

  • REAL mode → Fe2O3 lesson → Run → slow crystal growth, no bubbles, no flame but still exothermic
  • Compare to Mg burning (teacher demo bright white, fast oxidation)

Physical (week-long)

  • Put steel wool in cup with water, leave open, observe orange brown forming over days
  • Mass increases? (adds O and H₂O)

Lesson 5: Molecular Shape — VSEPR — Build H₂O and CH₄

GREEN — No chemicals, just building.

Objective

Valence determines shape: lone pairs repel. H2O bent 104.5°, CH4 tetrahedral 109.5°.

App Steps

  • Open v0.4.1 → REAL mode → Periodic Table → Click O (valence 2, 2 lone pairs) + H (1)
  • Builder (next update) or discuss: H-O-H bent because lone pairs push bonds together
  • C: valence 4 → 4 bonds → tetrahedral, all 109.5° apart — methane CH4
  • Show in app: Formula, Lewis (dots), 3D

Activity

  • Students use marshmallows/toothpicks or app builder to make H2O, CO2 linear 180°, NH3 trigonal pyramidal
  • Why water is polar? Bent + O more electronegative → partial charges

NGSS

PS1.A: Structure and Properties — valence and VSEPR.

Appendix — Safety & Standards

General Safety Checklist (for every lesson)

  • Goggles on, hair tied, no eating/drinking
  • Teacher handles dilute acids/bases (0.1M max), students GREEN only
  • Ventilation, wash hands after
  • No concentrated acids/bases, no flammable scale beyond teacher demo, no explosives, no illicit
  • App simulates only — physical demos optional with training

Why Dual Mode?

Real mode teaches real concepts safely. Fictional mode (Ae, Vy, Qx) lets kids experience excitement of plasma and crystal growth with high-energy visuals (+450kJ fictional) without mapping to dangerous real synthesis. You get the drama safely.
